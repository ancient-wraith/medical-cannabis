# My Medical Cannabis Treatment Goals

This page describes what I want to understand about my prescribed medical cannabis treatment.

My aim is to identify which flowers, if any, appear to suit my needs at different times of the day. I want to look beyond the immediate effects and consider my mood, mental health, pain, sleep and ability to function.

Everyone responds differently to cannabis. These are personal goals for monitoring my experience, not assumptions about what any particular product will do.

## 1. Depression and Low Mood

Depression is one of my main areas of interest.

I want to understand whether medical cannabis appears to affect my mood and general sense of wellbeing, rather than judging a product solely by whether it makes me feel relaxed or produces a temporary high.

Things I want to monitor:

- General mood and feelings of low mood.
- Motivation to start and complete everyday tasks.
- Interest in hobbies and activities.
- Ability to enjoy things I would normally enjoy.
- Energy levels and feelings of fatigue.
- Feelings of hopelessness or emotional flatness.
- Whether any changes continue beyond the immediate effects.
- Whether I notice changes in mood the following day.

**Questions I want to explore**

- Do I notice a meaningful difference in my mood after using a particular flower?
- Does feeling temporarily uplifted translate into any noticeable improvement in my everyday life?
- Do some products leave me feeling emotionally flat, unmotivated or less engaged?
- Are there differences between daytime and evening use?
- Do any perceived benefits continue into the following day?

I want to distinguish between the immediate subjective effects of THC and any broader changes in mood or day-to-day functioning.

## 2. Anxiety

I want to understand whether different flowers help me feel calmer or instead increase feelings of anxiety, unease or overstimulation.

Things I want to monitor:

- General anxiety levels.
- Feelings of tension or restlessness.
- Racing thoughts or difficulty switching off.
- Feeling relaxed without becoming excessively sleepy.
- Any increase in worry, nervousness or unease.
- Whether anxiety changes as the effects wear off.

**Questions I want to explore**

- Do I notice differences in anxiety between products?
- Does the THC concentration appear to make a difference to my experience?
- Can I feel calmer while remaining alert and able to concentrate?
- Do I experience anxiety during the onset, peak or offset of the effects?

THC can reduce anxiety for some people in some circumstances but can also increase it. I don't want to assume that a higher THC percentage will provide better relief.

## 3. Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD)

I want to pay attention to changes in mood, motivation and energy that may be associated with the seasons.

Because seasonal changes occur over longer periods, I need to be careful not to attribute every improvement or deterioration to medical cannabis.

Things I want to monitor:

- Seasonal changes in mood.
- Motivation and willingness to do things.
- Energy levels and daytime sleepiness.
- Interest in hobbies and social activities.
- Changes in sleep patterns.
- Whether low mood persists regardless of which flower I use.

**Questions I want to explore**

- Does my experience of medical cannabis change during different seasons?
- Do I notice any consistent changes in mood or motivation?
- Are there seasonal changes that appear unrelated to the products I'm using?

I will treat these as observations rather than evidence that cannabis treats SAD. I also want to remain aware of other factors, including daylight exposure, sleep, activity and my wider treatment.

## 4. Concentration and Mental Clarity

A product might help me relax but still be unsuitable for daytime use if it interferes with concentration.

I want to understand how different flowers affect my ability to think clearly and carry out everyday activities.

Things I want to monitor:

- Concentration and attention.
- Mental clarity.
- Memory and recall.
- Motivation to complete tasks.
- Mental fog or confusion.
- Sleepiness or reduced alertness.

**Questions I want to explore**

- Can I remain reasonably clear-headed after using a particular flower?
- Are some products more sedating than others?
- Does the dose or timing appear to affect concentration?
- Do I feel mentally clear again once the immediate effects have worn off?

Feeling relaxed is not necessarily the same as functioning better. I want to record both.

## 5. Bone Pain

Pain relief is another important treatment goal.

I want to understand whether different flowers provide noticeable relief, how long any perceived relief lasts and whether evening use appears to influence pain the next day.

Things I want to monitor:

- Pain before use.
- Pain after use.
- How soon I notice any change.
- How long the perceived relief lasts.
- Whether movement or everyday activities become easier.
- Whether pain returns as the effects wear off.
- Pain levels the following morning and during the following day.

**Questions I want to explore**

- Which products, if any, appear to provide useful relief?
- Does the timing of use make a difference?
- Do I notice a relationship between evening use and next-day pain?
- Does pain relief come with unwanted sedation or other effects?

I want to consider practical improvements, such as being more comfortable or finding daily activities easier, rather than relying on a single pain score.

## 6. Nerve Pain

I want to record nerve pain separately from bone pain because the symptoms and my experience of relief may differ.

Things I want to monitor:

- Overall nerve pain intensity.
- Burning, shooting, tingling or electric-shock sensations, where applicable.
- Changes in the frequency or intensity of symptoms.
- How soon I notice any change after use.
- How long any perceived relief lasts.
- Symptoms the following day.
- Any unwanted effects that outweigh the perceived benefit.

**Questions I want to explore**

- Do I notice a consistent difference in nerve pain with particular flowers?
- Does evening use appear to affect symptoms the next day?
- Are changes in nerve pain different from changes in bone pain?
- Does any perceived relief improve my ability to function?

I will record only the symptoms relevant to my own experience and avoid assuming that one product will affect every type of pain in the same way.

## 7. Sleep and Morning Wellbeing

THC often makes me sleepy, so I want to understand whether this is helpful at night and whether it causes problems at other times.

Things I want to monitor:

- How easily I fall asleep.
- How often I wake during the night.
- Overall sleep quality.
- Whether I feel rested in the morning.
- Morning grogginess or mental fog.
- Daytime sleepiness.
- Whether pain or mood affects my sleep.

**Questions I want to explore**

- Which products appear to help me settle at night?
- Does falling asleep more easily translate into better-quality sleep?
- Do I feel refreshed or groggy the next morning?
- Does better sleep coincide with changes in pain or mood?

Feeling sleepy is not necessarily the same as achieving restorative sleep, so I want to consider the following morning as well as the night itself.

## 8. Daytime and Evening Priorities

My priorities may differ depending on when I use a flower.

| Area | Daytime priority | Evening priority |
|---|---|---|
| Depression and mood | Mood, motivation and enjoyment | Evening mood and next-day wellbeing |
| Anxiety | Feeling calmer while remaining alert | Relaxation without unwanted anxiety |
| SAD | Mood, energy and motivation | Sleep and mood the following day |
| Concentration | Mental clarity and attention | Ability to unwind |
| Bone pain | Relief without excessive sleepiness | Pain relief and possible next-day effects |
| Nerve pain | Symptom relief and daily functioning | Symptom relief and possible next-day effects |
| Sleep | Avoid unwanted daytime sedation | Sleep quality and morning wellbeing |

These are priorities rather than guarantees. A flower that seems suitable for one purpose may not suit another.

## 9. Product Characteristics I Want to Compare

Alongside my symptoms and experiences, I want to record relevant product information.

- Product and strain name.
- Batch or prescription details.
- THC and CBD percentages.
- Amount used and time of use.
- Indica, Sativa or Hybrid label.
- Aroma and taste.
- Irradiation status, if known.
- Pack size and total cost.
- Cost per gram.
- Any unwanted effects.

I want to see whether any product characteristics correspond with differences in my experience. I recognise that strain labels, THC percentages, aroma and terpene profiles cannot reliably predict an individual's response on their own.

## 10. How I'll Assess My Experience

I want to avoid judging a product from a single session.

Where practical, I'll record how I feel before use, what happens afterwards and how I feel the following morning or day.

I'll look for patterns across multiple entries, while considering other influences such as sleep, stress, activity, season and changes in my general health.

I will not deliberately increase my dose or continue using a product that causes concerning effects just to complete a comparison. I'll follow my prescribing clinician's instructions and discuss significant side effects or treatment changes with them.

### What would count as a useful result?

A useful result does not have to mean that every symptom improves.

It might mean identifying a product that appears to provide pain relief without excessive sedation, or one that I tolerate better during the day. It might also mean discovering that a product does not suit me.

For depression, anxiety and SAD in particular, I want to focus on meaningful changes in mood and everyday functioning, not just whether I enjoy the immediate effects.

## 11. Review

When I review my diary, I'll ask myself:

- Have I noticed any consistent changes in depression or low mood?
- Have I noticed any consistent changes in anxiety?
- Have I noticed changes in motivation, enjoyment or energy?
- Have I noticed changes in bone pain or nerve pain?
- Does evening use appear to correspond with next-day pain or mood changes?
- How does each product affect concentration and sleep?
- Which unwanted effects have I experienced?
- Does the perceived benefit justify the cost?
- What questions should I discuss with my prescribing clinician?

The aim is to build a clearer picture of my own experience over time, without assuming that every change is caused by medical cannabis.
