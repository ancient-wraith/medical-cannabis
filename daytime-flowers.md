# Daytime Medical Cannabis Flowers

My aim is to research flowers that may be suitable for daytime use, with particular attention to depression, low mood, anxiety, motivation, enjoyment and concentration.

I want to find out whether any products appear to support my mental wellbeing while allowing me to remain reasonably alert and clear-headed.

This is not simply a search for the strongest or most uplifting flower. I want to understand the overall experience, including unwanted effects and how I feel later in the day.

## My Daytime Priorities

My priorities are:

- Depression and low mood.
- Motivation and enjoyment of everyday activities.
- Anxiety and feelings of tension.
- Concentration and mental clarity.
- Energy levels and daytime sleepiness.
- Bone pain and nerve pain without excessive sedation.
- Effects that continue after the immediate high has worn off.
- Mood, motivation and pain the following day.
- Cost and value for money.

I want to distinguish between a temporary change in how I feel while experiencing a high and a meaningful improvement in my everyday wellbeing.

## What About Depression?

Depression is one of my main reasons for investigating the effects of different flowers.

I want to be careful about what I measure. Feeling relaxed, experiencing a pleasant high or temporarily forgetting my problems is not necessarily the same as improving depression.

When recording my experience, I want to consider:

- General mood and low mood.
- Motivation to do things.
- Interest in hobbies and activities.
- Ability to enjoy everyday life.
- Energy levels.
- Feelings of emotional flatness.
- Whether any changes continue into the following day.

I also want to record whether a product makes me less motivated, more anxious or too sleepy to do things.

There is not enough reliable evidence to conclude that a particular strain name, THC percentage or Indica/Sativa classification will treat depression. I will treat any perceived benefits as personal observations to discuss with my clinician, rather than proof of an antidepressant effect.

## What About Anxiety?

THC can have different effects on anxiety depending on the person, the amount used and other factors.

Some people report feeling relaxed, while others experience increased anxiety, panic or unease.

I want to record whether I feel calmer without becoming excessively sleepy or mentally foggy.

A product that feels pleasant during one session may not produce the same effect every time. I will pay attention to unwanted effects as well as perceived benefits.

## THC, CBD and Daytime Functioning

THC is the main intoxicating cannabinoid in cannabis. Its effects can include changes in mood and perception, as well as drowsiness, anxiety and impaired concentration.

CBD is not intoxicating in the same way as THC, but its presence does not guarantee that a product will prevent THC-related unwanted effects.

I want to compare my experience of balanced and THC-dominant flowers without assuming that either formulation will be better for me.

I will follow my prescribed instructions rather than increasing THC concentration or changing my dose to chase a particular effect.

## Indica, Sativa and Hybrid

These labels are commonly used to describe cannabis flowers, but they are not reliable predictors of an individual's response.

A product labelled Sativa is not guaranteed to be energising. An Indica-labelled product is not guaranteed to be sedating, and a Hybrid label does not establish that the effects will be balanced.

I will record the label as product information, but I will judge my experience by what I actually notice.

## Aroma and Terpenes

Terpenes contribute to the aroma and flavour of cannabis. Product descriptions sometimes associate particular terpenes with relaxation, alertness or other effects.

These associations should not be treated as guarantees of clinical benefit. The effects of a whole flower cannot reliably be predicted from its aroma or terpene profile alone.

If reliable batch-specific information is available, I may record it alongside my observations.

## Cost and Irradiation

Price and irradiation status are important practical details for my research.

For each product, I want to record:

- Pack size.
- Total price.
- Price per gram.
- Pharmacy or supplier.
- Irradiated, non-irradiated or unknown.
- Source of the irradiation information.

Irradiation is used to reduce microbial contamination. Irradiation status alone does not establish whether a product is more effective, better tolerated or higher quality.

I will confirm both price and irradiation status with the pharmacy rather than relying on an old online listing.

## Reviewing My Results

I don't want to decide that a product works after a single session.

When reviewing my notes, I'll look for repeated patterns and consider other influences, including sleep, stress, activity, seasonal changes and fluctuations in my symptoms.

I also want to distinguish between:

- A temporary change during the high.
- A change that lasts for several hours.
- A perceived effect on the following day.
- A repeated pattern across different occasions.

Even a repeated pattern does not prove that cannabis caused the change, but it may help me ask better questions and have a more informed conversation with my prescribing clinician.

## Safety

I will use medical cannabis only as prescribed and follow the advice of my prescribing clinician and dispensing pharmacy.

THC can impair attention, judgement, coordination and reaction time. I will not drive or operate machinery while impaired, and I will follow UK rules concerning driving and prescribed cannabis.

If I experience significant worsening of anxiety or mood, troublesome side effects or other concerning symptoms, I will seek appropriate clinical advice.

I will not replace or discontinue established treatment for depression, anxiety or SAD without discussing it with my healthcare professional.
