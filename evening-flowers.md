# Evening Medical Cannabis Flowers

My aim is to research medical cannabis flowers that may be suitable for evening use, particularly in relation to bone pain, nerve pain, relaxation and sleep.

I also want to understand how evening use relates to my mental health, including depression, anxiety, motivation and mood the following day.

THC often makes me sleepy, so sedation may be something I notice in the evening. However, I want to distinguish between feeling sleepy, sleeping well and waking up feeling refreshed.

This page is a record of products I want to research, not a recommendation or a prediction of how they will affect me.

## My Evening Priorities

My main priorities are:

- Bone pain relief.
- Nerve pain relief.
- Relaxation and comfort.
- Sleep quality.
- How I feel the following morning.
- Depression and mood.
- Anxiety and emotional wellbeing.
- Next-day pain and mental clarity.
- Unwanted effects.
- Cost and value for money.

I want to look at the overall experience rather than judging a product solely by its THC percentage or how sleepy it makes me.

## An Observation I Want to Investigate

I've noticed that using medical cannabis for pain in the evening seems to help my pain during the following day.

I want to record this consistently and see whether the pattern continues.

There are several possibilities I want to keep in mind:

- I may experience pain relief that continues beyond the immediate effects.
- Better sleep may contribute to how I feel the following day.
- My symptoms may naturally fluctuate.
- Other factors, including activity, stress and sleep quality, may influence my pain.
- The apparent relationship may vary between products or occasions.

I also want to record my mood the following day. If I notice a change in depression, motivation or anxiety, I want to capture it rather than assuming that evening use affects only pain and sleep.

These are personal observations to investigate, not proof that evening cannabis use causes next-day improvements.

## Relaxation and Sleep

THC often makes me sleepy, which is relevant to my evening research.

However, I want to distinguish between sedation and sleep quality.

For example, feeling sleepy after using a flower does not necessarily mean I have slept well or woken up refreshed.

I'll consider:

- How easily I settle at night.
- How long it seems to take me to fall asleep.
- How often I wake during the night.
- My overall impression of sleep quality.
- How I feel when I wake up.
- Whether I experience morning grogginess.
- Whether I feel excessively sleepy the following day.

I also want to record whether pain or low mood interferes with my sleep, since these factors may influence the results.

I'll record:

## THC Strength and Product Labels

The products listed here have previously been described as THC-dominant flowers.

THC can produce intoxicating effects and may cause drowsiness, anxiety, impaired concentration and other unwanted effects.

A higher THC percentage does not necessarily mean a product will provide better pain relief or improve sleep. Individual responses vary, and strain names and Indica/Sativa labels are not reliable predictors of effects.

I want to focus on what I actually experience and follow my prescribed instructions rather than choosing a product simply because it has a higher THC percentage.

## Reviewing My Results

I want to look for patterns across multiple entries rather than drawing conclusions from one evening.

- Does a particular flower repeatedly appear to help with bone pain?

- Does evening use correspond with lower pain the following day?

## Safety

I will use medical cannabis only as prescribed and follow my clinician's and dispensing pharmacy's advice.

THC can impair attention, judgement, coordination and reaction time. I will not drive or operate machinery while impaired, and I will follow UK rules concerning driving and prescribed cannabis.

If I experience significant worsening of pain or mood, troublesome side effects or other concerning symptoms, I will seek appropriate clinical advice.

I will not stop or replace other treatment for depression, anxiety, pain or sleep without discussing it with my healthcare professional.
